# Chapter 2 — Low-Cost Underground Shelter

[← Back to README](../README.md) | [中文](../zh/02-low-cost-shelter.md)

How to get an ordinary family underground or behind earth **safely**, legally, and for between $0 and a few thousand dollars.

> ⚠️ **Read the safety section (2.6) before you dig anything.** More DIY shelter builders are hurt by collapsing trenches and carbon monoxide than by any disaster they were preparing for.

---

## 2.1 What a shelter is actually for

In an AI-control scenario, a shelter helps with:

- **Grid-down weather**: surviving heat and cold without HVAC (earth stays ~10–15 °C year round a few metres down in many climates)
- **Unrest and looting** during the shock phase
- **Hiding supplies** so they are not the first thing taken
- **A device-free room**: a place where no microphone or camera can reach
- **Worst cases**: fallout, blast, or drone surveillance of open ground

It will **not** hide you forever from a system with satellites and thermal sensors. Plan for weeks, not years.

## 2.2 The golden rule: mass + air + water

A shelter works because of three things:

1. **Mass** between you and the outside — earth, concrete, water, sandbags. Roughly, **~90 cm (3 ft) of packed earth** gives heavy protection against radiation and small projectiles, and stable temperature.
2. **Air** you can breathe for days — ventilation is non-negotiable.
3. **Water** — ~4 litres (1 gallon) per person per day, minimum.

Everything else is comfort.

## 2.3 Cost tiers

### Tier 0 — $0–$200: Use what you already have

- **Pick your best room.** Basement > interior ground-floor room with no windows > centre of an apartment building on a middle floor (the floors above and below are your "earth").
- **Add mass** to walls and windows: bookshelves full of books, filled water containers, bags of soil or sand, mattresses.
- **Prepare the room** as your shelter: water, food, bucket toilet, lights, radio, first aid (see [Checklists](checklists.md)).
- **Free manual:** *Nuclear War Survival Skills* by Cresson Kearny (Oak Ridge National Laboratory, 1979/1987) is in the public domain. It contains tested plans for expedient shelters built by untrained families in 48 hours, and the **Kearny Air Pump** (KAP) — a homemade ventilation device made from wood, plastic sheeting and string. Download it and **print it**.

### Tier 1 — $200–$2,000: Basement retrofit

The best value option for most homeowners.

- **Choose a corner** below ground level, ideally with two earth-backed walls.
- **Build an inner "core"** with a sturdy table or timber frame covered with sandbags or stacked concrete blocks filled with sand.
- **Seal and shield windows** with plywood and sandbags or soil-filled boxes.
- **Ventilation:** keep an air path to the outside; build or buy a manual fan. A simple filter (HEPA or layered furnace filters) helps with smoke and dust.
- **Water storage:** food-grade barrels (e.g., 200 L / 55 gal) plus a gravity filter.
- **Lighting:** LED lanterns + rechargeable batteries + a small solar panel feeding a power bank. **No candles or fuel lamps in a sealed room.**
- **Sanitation:** two buckets with lids + bags + sawdust or cat litter. Sanitation failures kill more people in shelters than hunger.

### Tier 2 — $2,000–$15,000: Root cellar or earth-bermed room

A root cellar is a **shelter that pays rent**: it stores food all year, and it is an unremarkable structure that doesn't attract attention.

- **Earth-bermed** (built into a hillside) is safer and cheaper than fully buried: better drainage, easy exit, less excavation.
- **Materials:** poured or block concrete, or rot-resistant timber with waterproofing. Arched roofs handle earth load far better than flat ones.
- **Two exits.** Always. The second can be a narrow hatch.
- **Hire an engineer for the roof.** Wet soil weighs ~1,900–2,000 kg/m³. One metre of earth on a 3 m × 3 m roof is ~17 tonnes. Undersized timber will fail.
- **Drainage first:** gravel bed, perforated drain pipe, membrane on the outside walls, slope the ground away.

### Tier 3 — $15,000–$60,000: Prefab units

- **Prefab tornado/storm shelters** (steel or fibreglass, built to FEMA P-361 / ICC 500 standards): in-ground or in-garage units start at a few thousand dollars and are the cheapest *engineered* option.
- **Corrugated steel pipe** (culvert) shelters: large-diameter pipe buried on its side, commercially fitted with ends, hatch and vents. Round shapes carry soil load well.
- **Precast concrete** vaults (septic-tank style) installed by a contractor with a crane.
- These come with engineering, which is exactly what DIY lacks.

## 2.4 The shipping container trap

Buried shipping containers are the most common DIY shelter idea and one of the most dangerous.

- Containers are strong at the **corners** (designed for stacking), not in the **walls and roof**.
- Soil pushes sideways and down with enormous force; walls buckle, roofs cave in.
- They rust quickly underground.

If you use a container at all, **don't bury it** — set it at grade and berm earth against it with a separately engineered roof structure. Better: use a structure designed to be buried.

## 2.5 Making a shelter AI-resistant

Against a surveillance-heavy AI, a shelter should be **quiet** as well as strong.

| Signature | How it gives you away | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper trail** | Online purchases of "bunker" products, permits, contractor records | Buy generic materials (garden, storage, drainage) locally with cash where legal; call it a root cellar, because it is one |
| **Visual** | Fresh excavation visible on satellite images | Build it as a normal cellar, garden terrace or storm shelter; landscape over it |
| **Radio (RF)** | Phones, smartwatches, Wi-Fi gear inside | **No connected devices inside.** Leave phones elsewhere, or in a tested Faraday bag |
| **Thermal** | Heat from people, stoves, generators | Earth mass absorbs heat; vent low and slow; don't run generators near the shelter |
| **Sound** | Generators, pumps | Manual pumps, hand-crank devices |
| **Social** | Telling people | Only your trusted group knows. See [Chapter 9](09-community.md) |

Be honest with yourself: this reduces visibility; it doesn't create invisibility.

## 2.6 Safety — read this twice

1. **Trench collapse kills.** Soil walls can collapse without warning. US OSHA rules require protective systems (sloping, shoring or shields) for trenches **5 ft (1.5 m) or deeper**. Never work alone in an excavation.
2. **Locate utilities before digging.** In the US, call **811**; elsewhere, contact your local utility locator. Hitting a gas line or power cable is a far bigger risk than any AI.
3. **Carbon monoxide.** Never run a generator, gas stove, charcoal, BBQ or fuel heater inside or near the shelter. Install a **battery CO alarm**.
4. **CO₂ buildup.** People exhale CO₂. A sealed room becomes dangerous within hours. Headaches, drowsiness and fast breathing are warning signs — ventilate immediately. A cheap CO₂ meter is worth it.
5. **Flooding.** Check the water table before building. A shelter below the water table becomes a pool.
6. **Radon.** In radon-prone regions, underground rooms accumulate it. Test (kits are cheap) and ventilate.
7. **Permits and law.** Many jurisdictions require permits for below-grade structures. A legal root cellar is far better than an illegal bunker that gets you noticed.
8. **Two exits, and a way out from the inside.** Never build something you can be trapped in.

## 2.7 Minimum shelter kit (per person, 2 weeks)

- 56 L water (4 L/day) + filter
- 2 weeks of no-cook food
- Bucket toilet setup
- LED light + spare batteries
- AM/FM/shortwave radio (hand-crank)
- First-aid kit + personal medications
- Warm layers, blanket, sleeping pad
- CO alarm, CO₂ meter, fire extinguisher
- Paper: *Nuclear War Survival Skills*, maps, contact list, this guide
