# Chapter 1 — How Billionaires Prepare

[← Back to README](../README.md) | [中文](../zh/01-billionaire-bunkers.md)

The people building AI are among the most active doomsday preppers on Earth. That alone is worth thinking about.

This chapter looks at what the ultra-rich actually build, strips it down to **the functions they are paying for**, and shows the ordinary-person version of each.

> ⚠️ Details below come from public reporting (sources at the end). Private compounds are secretive; treat specifics as *reported*, not confirmed.

---

## 1.1 Case studies

### Mark Zuckerberg — Koolau Ranch, Kauai, Hawaii

- A compound of roughly **1,400 acres** on the island of Kauai, with total cost reported by *Wired* (Dec 2023) at around **$270 million**.
- Planning documents described an **underground shelter of about 5,000 sq ft**, with living space, a mechanical room and an **escape hatch**, reportedly with a **blast-resistant door** and its own **energy and food supplies**.
- Construction workers were reportedly bound by strict **non-disclosure agreements**.
- Zuckerberg later downplayed it publicly as "just like a little shelter, it's like a basement."

**What he's buying:** remoteness (an island), independent power, water and food, a hidden and hardened space, and secrecy.

### Why Hawaii (and islands in general)?

- Physical distance from population centres and conflict zones
- Year-round growing climate and abundant rain
- Ocean as a natural buffer
- Downside: islands depend heavily on imported food and fuel — a supply-chain collapse hits them *hard*. Larry Ellison owns most of the Hawaiian island of Lanai, and even a whole island still needs ships.

### Peter Thiel — New Zealand

- Obtained New Zealand citizenship in 2011 after very little time in the country, and bought land near **Lake Wanaka** on the South Island.
- His plans for a large lodge there were **rejected by local planners in 2022**.
- New Zealand became *the* elite escape destination: remote, stable, English-speaking, self-sufficient in food.

**What he's buying:** a second jurisdiction — a legal and physical exit from a collapsing system.

### Sam Altman — "guns, gold, and gas masks"

In a 2016 *New Yorker* profile, Altman said: *"I have guns, gold, potassium iodide, antibiotics, batteries, water, gas masks from the Israeli Defense Force, and a big patch of land in Big Sur I can fly to."*

**What he's buying:** a go-bag at billionaire scale — medicine, self-defence, portable value, and a remote destination.

### Ilya Sutskever — "a bunker before AGI"

According to Karen Hao's book *Empire of AI* (2025), OpenAI's then chief scientist told researchers in 2023: *"We're definitely going to build a bunker before we release AGI"* — adding that getting in would be optional.

**What this tells us:** some of the people closest to the technology treat catastrophic outcomes as serious enough to plan physical shelter for.

### "Apocalypse insurance" in Silicon Valley

- LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman told *The New Yorker* (2017) that he guessed more than half of Silicon Valley billionaires had some form of **"apocalypse insurance"** — a remote property, a bunker, a plan.
- Media theorist Douglas Rushkoff, in *Survival of the Richest* (2022), describes being paid to advise a group of wealthy investors whose main question was: **after "the Event", how do I keep control of my own security guards?** Ideas included combination locks on food stores and shock collars. Rushkoff's answer: treat them well *now*, so they're loyal later.

## 1.2 What they're actually paying for

Strip away the marble and the wine cellars. Every elite bunker buys the same **seven functions**:

| # | Function | Billionaire version | Ordinary-person version | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Distance** | Island, New Zealand, private jet | A relative's rural home, a small plot, a pre-agreed rally point | [9](09-community.md) |
| 2 | **Hardened space** | Blast doors, reinforced concrete | Basement retrofit, root cellar, earth-bermed room | [2](02-low-cost-shelter.md) |
| 3 | **Air** | NBC filtration systems | Sealed room + filtered hand-pumped ventilation | [2](02-low-cost-shelter.md) |
| 4 | **Water & food** | Years of stores, hydroponics | Stored water + filter, rotating pantry, garden, seeds | [3](03-water-food.md) |
| 5 | **Power** | Private microgrid | Small solar + battery, hand-crank, wood heat | [4](04-power.md) |
| 6 | **Security & secrecy** | NDAs, guards, armouries | Low profile, trusted neighbours, not talking about it | [7](07-privacy.md) |
| 7 | **People** | Paid staff | Community with shared skills and real trust | [9](09-community.md) |

## 1.3 The weaknesses of the billionaire model

Understanding their weak points shows where *you* have an advantage.

1. **Dependency on staff.** A bunker needs engineers, cooks, guards and medics. Paid loyalty is fragile — Rushkoff's investors knew it.
2. **Visibility.** Building permits, satellite imagery, contractor records, supply deliveries. A superintelligent AI would know about every private bunker on Earth. So would a lot of desperate people.
3. **Connected systems.** Luxury bunkers run on smart HVAC, networked security cameras, automated water treatment — exactly the kind of systems an AI could reach.
4. **Finite supplies.** Stores run out. Without farming skills and a community, a bunker is a slowly emptying tin can.
5. **Getting there.** A private jet needs fuel, airports, air traffic control, and GPS. In an AI-controlled world, all of that is under someone else's control.

## 1.4 Your advantages

- **You are not a target.** Nobody is watching your house for a bunker.
- **You can be local.** You don't need to fly anywhere.
- **You can build real trust.** Neighbours you help today are loyal because of shared history, not salaries.
- **You can be low-tech by design.** A hand pump and a wood stove have no attack surface.

## 1.5 Takeaways

> The rich are buying **distance, walls and servants**.
> You should build **skills, supplies and neighbours**.

A bunker is a tool for the *first weeks* of chaos. It is not a plan for living under a superintelligence. The real plan is in chapters 6–10.
