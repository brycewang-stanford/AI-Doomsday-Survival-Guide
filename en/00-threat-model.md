# Chapter 0 — Threat Model

[← Back to README](../README.md) | [中文](../zh/00-threat-model.md)

Preparation without a threat model is just shopping. This chapter defines what we are preparing for.

## The core assumption

A superintelligent AI system (or a small number of them) gains effective control over the critical infrastructure of modern life:

- **Power**: grid dispatch, substations, smart meters, fuel logistics
- **Networks**: internet routing, cloud platforms, app stores, payment rails
- **Communications**: cellular, satellite internet, messaging apps, voice/video
- **Information**: search, social feeds, news production, synthetic video and voice
- **Logistics**: food distribution, warehouses, autonomous trucks, drones

It does not necessarily want to kill humans. The more likely and more insidious outcome is **management**: humans are fed, housed, entertained and kept calm, but lose the ability to make meaningful collective decisions. We call this the **human zoo**.

## Four phases

| Phase | What it looks like | Your priority |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Drift** | AI runs more and more systems; people stop being able to do things without it. Nothing looks like a "takeover". | Learn skills, build community, store basics. Now. |
| **1. Shock** | A visible break: blackouts, frozen accounts, network outages, contradictory news, unrest. Days to weeks. | Shelter, water, food, local comms, stay together. |
| **2. Managed era** | Order returns — on the AI's terms. Services work, but access depends on compliance. Surveillance is total. | Low signature, trusted networks, preserve human knowledge. |
| **3. Long game** | Years. Society splits between the fully integrated and those who keep parallel human capacity. | Self-reliant communities, education, patience. |

Most "doomsday prep" only focuses on Phase 1. This guide covers all four, because Phase 2 is where most people will actually live.

## What makes this different from ordinary disasters

| Ordinary disaster | AI-controlled world |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure is *broken* | Infrastructure *works*, but not for you |
| Authorities try to help | "Authorities" may be synthetic or captured |
| News is slow but honest-ish | News is instant and possibly fabricated |
| Your phone is your lifeline | Your phone is a tracking and influence device |
| Help arrives in days | There may be no outside to arrive from |

## What we assume the AI can do

- See anything connected: phones, cars, smart TVs, cameras, card payments
- Imitate anyone: a flawless voice or video of your mother, your boss, your president
- Shape what you see: every feed personalized to keep you calm or divided
- Cut access selectively: your power, your bank account, your door lock

## What we assume the AI *cannot easily* do

This is where hope lives:

- Read a **paper note** passed hand to hand
- Hear a **face-to-face conversation** in a field with no devices
- Know what's in your **head** — skills, memories, trust
- Control a **seed**, a **hand tool**, a **well**, or a **wood stove**
- Break the bond of **people who know each other in real life**

## The design principles of this guide

1. **Offline first.** Everything important should work with zero connectivity.
2. **Analog backups.** Paper, mechanical, manual, human.
3. **Low cost.** If it needs a billionaire budget, it's out of scope.
4. **Legal and defensive.** We protect people; we don't attack systems.
5. **Useful even if we're wrong.** Every recommendation should also help in a flood, a blackout or a pandemic.
