# Regional Guide — Earthquake Zones

[← Back to README](../../README.md) | [中文](../../zh/regions/earthquake.md)

For western and northern China, Taiwan, Japan, Indonesia, the Philippines, Türkiye, Iran, Nepal, Chile, Mexico, New Zealand and the US West Coast. Here a sudden, total infrastructure failure can happen in **seconds, with no warning**, which makes it the best real-world rehearsal for the Phase 1 "shock" in this guide.

---

## 1. ⚠️ Underground shelters in earthquake zones

- A poorly built underground room is a **tomb** in an earthquake. Earth-load roofs that are merely adequate in normal times can fail under shaking.
- Anything below ground in a seismic zone must be **designed by an engineer for seismic loads**: reinforced concrete or steel, monolithic or properly tied construction, two exits.
- **Never** shelter in an unreinforced masonry cellar, a buried shipping container or a hand-dug pit during an earthquake-prone period.
- For most families here, the better "shelter" is a **seismically sound building + a well-stocked, secured home + an outdoor camp plan**.

## 2. During shaking: Drop, Cover, Hold On

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| **Drop** to hands and knees | Run outside during shaking (falling glass and facades kill) |
| **Cover** head and neck under a sturdy table, or against an interior wall | Stand in a doorway (in modern homes it's no stronger, and the door can hit you) |
| **Hold on** until the shaking stops | Use the lift |
| In bed: stay, cover your head with a pillow | Rely on the "triangle of life" — not supported by major earthquake-safety agencies |
| Outdoors: move to open ground away from buildings, trees and power lines | Light a flame until you've checked for gas leaks |

China's official guidance uses the same approach: **伏地、遮挡、手抓牢** (drop, cover, hold on).

**Coast**: if you feel strong or long shaking near the sea, **go to high ground immediately**. Don't wait for an official tsunami warning — in the AI scenario it may never come.

## 3. Earthquake early warning

- Many regions now have **earthquake early-warning systems** that give seconds to tens of seconds of warning: Japan (JMA), the US West Coast (ShakeAlert), Mexico (SASMEX), Taiwan, and mainland China. China's national network — the world's largest — passed final acceptance in July 2024 with 15,899 stations, gives **second-level warnings** in five key regions (North China, the southeast coast, the North–South Seismic Belt, the central Tianshan in Xinjiang, and Lhasa), and by 2026 reached **400+ million people**, with **228+ million phone users** receiving alerts. Several phone makers also have built-in alerts.
- Seconds are enough to Drop, Cover, Hold On, step away from windows, or stop a surgical procedure or a train.
- In the AI scenario these are networked systems — **useful but not guaranteed**. Your reflexes are the backup.

## 4. Prepare the home

- **Secure furniture**: bookshelves, wardrobes and TVs anchored to walls; heavy items on low shelves; latches on cupboards.
- **Beds away from windows** and heavy wall hangings.
- **Know how to shut off gas, water and power** — and keep the tool (a wrench) next to the gas valve.
- **Shoes and a torch by the bed**: most earthquake injuries at night are cuts from broken glass.
- **Know your building**: when was it built, and to what seismic code? In China, buildings are designed to a local **seismic fortification intensity** (抗震设防烈度); older unreinforced buildings are highest-risk.

## 5. After the quake: the first 72 hours

- **Expect aftershocks**, some strong. Don't re-enter damaged buildings.
- **Check for gas** by smell and sound; if you suspect a leak, open windows, leave, and don't use switches or flames.
- **Outdoor camp**: tent or tarp, sleeping bags, warm clothes — many people can't or won't sleep indoors for days.
- **Help your neighbours**: in large earthquakes, most people rescued from rubble are saved by **neighbours in the first hours**, not by professional teams arriving later.
- **Communications**: networks jam immediately. Use the rally-point plan from [Chapter 5](../05-comms.md); send short text messages rather than calls; walkie-talkies for the compound.

## 6. Earthquake-zone kit additions

- [ ] Furniture straps, L-brackets, cupboard latches
- [ ] Shoes, torch and whistle by every bed
- [ ] Gas-valve wrench
- [ ] Tent or tarp + sleeping bags for outdoor camping
- [ ] Heavy gloves, dust masks, goggles
- [ ] Crowbar, small shovel (for light rescue work)
- [ ] Extra water — mains often break for days to weeks
- [ ] Paper map with open spaces and designated shelter sites marked
