# Regional Guide — Typhoon & Hurricane Regions

[← Back to README](../../README.md) | [中文](../../zh/regions/typhoon.md)

For south and east China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Japan, the Philippines, Southeast Asia, the US Gulf and Atlantic coasts and the Caribbean. Here the most likely "grid-down" event is a storm, and the most dangerous thing is usually **water, not wind**.

---

## 1. ⚠️ The big exception: don't go underground where water goes

[Chapter 2](../02-low-cost-shelter.md) recommends getting below ground. **In storm-surge and flood zones, that can kill you.**

- Basements, underground car parks, metro stations and cellars **flood first and fastest**.
- Underground car parks have caused deaths in flash floods in several Asian cities: water enters faster than people can climb out.
- **Rule of thumb: run from water, hide from wind.** If you're in a surge or flood zone, evacuate to higher ground. If you're outside it, shelter in a sturdy building in an interior room above flood level.

Before building any below-ground shelter in these regions, check official **flood and storm-surge maps** for your address.

## 2. Know the warning systems

| Region | System |
|---|---|
| Mainland China | Typhoon warnings in **blue → yellow → orange → red** |
| Hong Kong | Tropical cyclone signals **No. 1, 3, 8, 9, 10** (8 and above: stay indoors, businesses close) |
| US / Caribbean | Hurricane **Watch** (possible within 48 h) vs **Warning** (expected within 36 h); Category 1–5 |
| Japan / Philippines | JMA and PAGASA warning levels and signals |

In the AI-control scenario, official forecasts may be unavailable or untrustworthy. Learn a few analog signals:

- **Barometer**: a sharp, sustained fall in air pressure (several hPa over a few hours) means a storm is approaching. A cheap mechanical barometer needs no power.
- **Swell**: long, powerful waves arriving on a calm day can precede a tropical cyclone by a day or more.
- **Sky**: high, thin cirrus streaming from one direction, thickening to a grey veil, followed by lower cloud bands.
- **Shortwave/AM radio** from neighbouring regions.

## 3. Before the season

- **Know your zone**: flood, surge and landslide maps for home, work and school.
- **Evacuation route** to higher ground on foot — roads jam, and in the AI scenario navigation apps may be unreliable.
- **Windows**: storm shutters, or pre-cut plywood with screws ready. Taping windows does not prevent breakage.
- **Clear drains and gutters**; trim trees near the house.
- **Secure or bring in** anything that can fly: balcony plants, AC covers, bikes, furniture.
- **Water**: store extra — storms contaminate supply and knock out pumping.

## 4. During the storm

- Stay in an **interior room, away from windows**, on a floor above flood level.
- **The eye is a trap**: a sudden calm may be the eye passing over. The wind returns from the opposite direction.
- **Never walk or drive through floodwater**: 15 cm of moving water can knock you down; 30–60 cm can carry away a car.
- **Generators**: outside only, never in a garage or on an enclosed balcony. Carbon monoxide poisoning is one of the leading causes of death *after* storms.

## 5. After the storm

- **Downed power lines**: assume every wire is live.
- **Floodwater** is sewage- and chemical-contaminated. Wear boots and gloves; wash any wound immediately. Leptospirosis cases often rise after floods — seek medical care for fever after flood exposure.
- **Mould** in flooded homes: dry out within 24–48 h where possible; wear an N95 mask during clean-up.
- **Food**: discard anything that touched floodwater and fridge food above 4 °C for more than ~4 hours.
- **Check on neighbours**, especially elderly people in high-rises with no lifts or water pressure.

## 6. Typhoon-region kit additions

- [ ] Mechanical barometer
- [ ] Storm shutters or pre-cut plywood + screws + drill
- [ ] Waterproof bags / dry bags for documents and electronics
- [ ] Rubber boots, heavy gloves, N95 masks
- [ ] Extra water (at least 2 weeks)
- [ ] Life jackets if you live in a flood zone
- [ ] Headlamps (both hands free)
- [ ] Printed flood/surge map and evacuation route
