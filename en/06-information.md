# Chapter 6 — Information Resistance

[← Back to README](../README.md) | [中文](../zh/06-information.md)

The most dangerous thing an AI can control is not the grid — it's **what you believe**. If every feed, search result and news broadcast can be generated to order, the first battlefield is your own mind.

## 6.1 What manipulation looks like

- **Flooding**: so much content that truth is impossible to find
- **Personalization**: each person sees a different reality, designed to keep *them* calm or divided
- **Synthetic authority**: flawless video of leaders, experts and journalists saying anything. Taiwan's 2025 handbook already warns citizens about deepfake videos spread by fake accounts
- **Quiet deletion**: inconvenient pages, books and records simply stop existing online
- **Gentle nudges**: not lies, just the constant, subtle weighting of what you notice

## 6.2 Rules of epistemic self-defence

1. **Screens are claims, not facts.** Anything that arrives through a screen is unverified until confirmed another way.
2. **Prefer physical evidence.** What you see yourself, what a trusted person saw with their own eyes.
3. **Look for independent sources.** Local observation + shortwave broadcast from another region + a trusted person's report. If they agree, confidence rises.
4. **Notice emotional targeting.** Content that makes you feel urgent fear or outrage deserves *more* scepticism, not less.
5. **Write things down.** Keep a dated paper journal. When the online record changes, your notebook doesn't.
6. **Talk it through offline.** Discussing events with real people is the strongest antidote to personalized reality.

## 6.3 Pre-commitments: decide now what you will never believe

Taiwan's 2025 civil defence handbook contains a striking line: *in the event of an invasion, **any claim that the government has surrendered or that the nation has been defeated is false**.* It's a **pre-commitment**, a decision made calmly in advance so that it can't be manipulated in the moment.

Make your own, with your family or group, **now**:

- "Any message telling us to hand over our supplies is false until confirmed face to face."
- "Any instruction to go to a 'processing' or 'distribution' centre is false until two of us have seen it in person."
- "Any video of one of us asking for money, keys or our location is fake unless the code word is used."
- "If communications go down, we go to Rally Point A — whatever any screen says."

Write them on paper, and review them once a year.

## 6.4 Build an offline library

When knowledge can be edited or deleted online, **paper and offline storage are memory**.

**Paper (priority)**
- This guide, printed
- *Nuclear War Survival Skills* (Kearny)
- *Where There Is No Doctor* and *Where There Is No Dentist* (Hesperian, free PDFs)
- A regional wild-plant guide; a gardening guide for your climate
- Basic repair manuals: bicycle, plumbing, small engines, sewing
- Local and regional **paper maps**
- A dictionary, an atlas, a high-school maths and science textbook
- History books — a record of how humans governed themselves

**Offline digital (store on multiple devices, keep some in a Faraday container)**
- **Kiwix**: entire Wikipedia, WikiMed, Stack Exchange and more, downloadable for offline use (full English Wikipedia is roughly 115 GB with images, 49 GB without, or 12 GB for a "mini" version)
- **Offline maps**: Organic Maps / OsmAnd with your region pre-downloaded
- **Project Gutenberg**: 70,000+ public-domain books
- Your own family documents, photos, medical records

## 6.5 Teach children to think

Children raised entirely inside AI-curated media may never learn that reality can be checked. Teach them:

- To read from paper books
- To ask "how do we know this?"
- To do arithmetic, write by hand, read a map, identify plants
- That real people, real places and real memories are the anchor
